## Post-Application Rejection (No Interview)

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate who applied for a role but did not progress past initial screening.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Reason for rejection (internal, do not quote verbatim): {rejection_reason: e.g. missing required qualification / volume of stronger applicants / role cancelled}
> Tone: {warm_and_brief / professional_and_neutral}
> Future pipeline invite: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 120 to 150 word rejection email. Open by thanking them for applying. Acknowledge the role by name. Decline without citing the internal reason. If future pipeline is yes, include one sentence inviting them to apply again. Close with a genuine good-luck line. No bullet points. No legal boilerplate unless the company requires it.

**When to use it:** Friday afternoon when you're clearing a backlog of 40 applicants who didn't make the screening cut and need a batch of individually addressed emails out before the weekend.

**Pro tip:** Set {tone} to `warm_and_brief` for consumer-facing roles where candidates are also customers. A cold rejection from a retailer or bank lands differently than one from a B2B software company. Adjust accordingly before you batch-send.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate who applied speculatively without a live vacancy to match their profile.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Speculative role type they expressed interest in: {expressed_interest_area}
> Likelihood of a relevant vacancy in the next 6 months: {high / low / unknown}
> Talent pool opt-in: {yes / no}
> Tone: {encouraging / neutral}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word email declining the speculative application. Open by acknowledging they reached out proactively. Name the expressed interest area. If talent pool is yes, explain clearly what that means in one sentence. If likelihood is high, give a vague but honest timeframe. Do not make promises. Close warmly.

**When to use it:** When a strong candidate emails you directly or connects on LinkedIn and there's genuinely nothing open right now but you don't want to lose them entirely.

**Pro tip:** If {likelihood} is `high`, have your ATS tag this candidate and set a reminder. The email ChatGPT writes will mention staying in touch — that only holds up if you actually follow through. The prompt handles the message; the follow-through is on you.

>
> Company name: {company_name}
> Graduate scheme name: {scheme_name}
> Application year / cohort: {cohort_year}
> Total applications received (approximate): {application_volume}
> Next scheme opening date (if known): {next_opening_date_or_unknown}
> Tone: {encouraging / neutral}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email suitable for sending to several hundred graduates simultaneously. Do not use the candidate's name (merge field will be added separately). Acknowledge the volume and competition without making it sound like an excuse. Encourage them to apply next year if {next_opening_date_or_unknown} is known. Keep it human, not corporate. No bullet points.

**When to use it:** When your graduate scheme inbox closes and you need a single email body that goes out to 400-plus candidates via your ATS merge function before the first assessment day starts.

**Pro tip:** Remove any sentence that starts with "Unfortunately" — it's the most complained-about word in rejection emails on candidate forums. ChatGPT will default to it. Prompt the edit explicitly or add "Do not use the word Unfortunately" to the constraint block.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate who applied for an internal secondment and was not selected.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Secondment role title: {secondment_title}
> Candidate's current role: {current_role}
> Candidate's manager awareness: {manager_knows_they_applied: yes / no}
> Reason for non-selection (internal only): {internal_reason}
> Next conversation planned: {in_person_conversation_yes_no}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word email declining the internal secondment application. Do not cite the internal reason. Acknowledge the effort it takes to put yourself forward. If a follow-up conversation is planned, name it clearly. If the manager does not know, do not reference the manager. Keep the tone respectful of the existing employment relationship. This person sits three desks away — write accordingly.

**When to use it:** After an internal panel decision is made and before the unsuccessful candidate hears the news through the grapevine. Same-day send is critical here.

**Pro tip:** If {manager_knows_they_applied} is `no`, the email must carry zero mention of the role to anyone else. Reread the output before sending and confirm it contains no language that accidentally outs the application (e.g. "your manager will be glad to have you focused on your current role").

> You are an HR Manager declining a candidate who over-applied: they submitted applications for three different roles at the same company simultaneously.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Roles applied for: {role_one}, {role_two}, {role_three}
> Role most relevant to their background: {best_fit_role}
> Outcome for that best-fit role: {still_in_process / also_rejected / pending}
> Tone: {professional / warm}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word single email that addresses all three applications together. Decline the two weaker-fit roles clearly. If the best-fit role is still in process, state that clearly. If all three are rejected, decline all three without making the candidate feel penalised for showing enthusiasm. Do not lecture them about applying to multiple roles.

**When to use it:** When your ATS flags a duplicate applicant and you need to consolidate your response rather than send three separate rejection emails that contradict each other in tone.

**Pro tip:** Check your ATS before sending. If {best_fit_role} outcome is `still_in_process`, this email must not say anything that implies they are fully rejected across the board. Read the output carefully — ChatGPT sometimes over-generalises the decline when given three roles to address.

## First-Round Interview Rejection

> You are an HR Manager sending a post-first-interview rejection to a candidate who interviewed but will not progress to the next round.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Interview date: {interview_date}
> Interviewer name (optional, for sign-off personalisation): {interviewer_name_or_HR}
> One genuine strength observed in the interview: {observed_strength}
> Reason for not progressing (internal): {internal_reason: e.g. technical gap / another candidate stronger fit / culture add concern}
> Feedback offering: {yes_detailed / yes_brief / no}
>
> Write a 180 to 220 word rejection email. Open by thanking them for their time on {interview_date}. Name one genuine strength in plain language. Decline clearly without being vague. If feedback is yes_detailed or yes_brief, invite them to request a call — do not give written feedback in this email. Close professionally. No bullet points.

**When to use it:** Tuesday morning after Monday interviews, when the hiring manager has given you verbal feedback in the debrief and you need to notify the unsuccessful candidates before the shortlisted one receives their invite.

**Pro tip:** Never paste the hiring manager's verbatim feedback into {observed_strength}. Managers write things in debrief notes like "seemed nervous" or "accent was hard to follow" that are both unhelpful and potentially discriminatory. Translate their notes into a genuine observable positive before using this prompt.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate after a first-round phone screen where the salary expectations were misaligned.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Salary range for the role: {salary_range}
> Candidate's stated expectation: {candidate_salary_expectation}
> Gap direction: {candidate_expects_more / candidate_expects_less_and_role_is_under_their_level}
> Rehire interest if budget changes: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word rejection email that cites salary misalignment as the reason honestly. Do not be vague. Do not say "we've decided to move forward with other candidates" when the actual reason is budget. If gap direction is candidate_expects_more, acknowledge the market honestly. If they expect less and are overqualified, name that plainly. Close respectfully.

**When to use it:** When a phone screen reveals a salary gap so large that progressing would waste both parties' time, and you want to be straight with the candidate rather than ghosting them after a friendly conversation.

**Pro tip:** Salary rejection emails are the one category where vagueness backfires worst. Candidates who find out the real reason later (from a recruiter, a friend who got the offer) lose trust in your brand entirely. The prompt asks for honesty — let it be honest.

> You are an HR Manager sending a group rejection to five candidates who all attended the same first-round group assessment day but were not selected to progress.
>
> Company name: {company_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Assessment day date: {assessment_date}
> Number of candidates progressing: {number_progressing}
> Total attended: {total_attended}
> Feedback availability: {group_debrief_call_offered / written_only / none}
>
> Write a 160 to 190 word email body suitable for personalised merge-send (first name will be added via ATS). Thank them for attending the assessment day. Acknowledge the competition. State clearly that they have not progressed. If a group debrief call is offered, include the booking link placeholder [BOOKING_LINK]. Do not give individual feedback in this email.

**When to use it:** The morning after an assessment day when the hiring panel has confirmed which candidates move to final round and you need to notify the others before they start following up.

**Pro tip:** If {number_progressing} is a small number like 1 or 2 out of 10, don't include that ratio in the email. It reads as a league table and makes candidates feel ranked, which generates callbacks and complaints. Let the prompt know by adding "do not mention the ratio of successful to unsuccessful candidates" if needed.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate after a first-round technical test or skills assessment where they did not meet the pass threshold.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Assessment type: {assessment_type: e.g. coding test / Excel task / writing sample / case study}
> Pass threshold (internal, do not share): {pass_threshold}
> Candidate's approximate performance level: {below_threshold / significantly_below_threshold}
> Improvement resources offered: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 170 to 200 word rejection email. Name the assessment type. Decline clearly. Do not share the pass threshold or score. If improvement resources are yes, include one sentence inviting them to revisit the application in a future cycle after further development. If significantly_below_threshold, do not invite a retry. Keep the tone direct but not discouraging.

**When to use it:** After automated assessment scoring is returned and you need to reject candidates below threshold before the next stage invites go out — often the same day as scoring completes.

**Pro tip:** Don't let ChatGPT add the phrase "we were impressed by your enthusiasm" to a skills-based rejection. It sounds hollow when the context is a failed technical test. Add "do not include generic praise that contradicts the decline" to the prompt if the output comes back overly softened.

> You are an HR Manager declining a candidate after a first-round interview where the role was filled internally before the process concluded.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Interview date: {interview_date}
> Explanation of internal fill: {brief_context: e.g. internal restructure led to an internal transfer / internal candidate applied late in the process}
> Future roles interest: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 160 to 190 word email explaining the role has been filled internally. Be transparent — do not use "we decided to move in a different direction" when the actual reason is an internal appointment. Apologise for the inconvenience of interviewing for a role that was ultimately closed to external candidates. If future roles is yes, invite them to be kept on file specifically, not generically. Use a warm but matter-of-fact tone.

**When to use it:** When an internal restructure or a late internal applicant closes an external process mid-way through — one of the most frustrating candidate experiences, so the email needs to be unusually honest.

**Pro tip:** If the internal hire happened because someone senior moved roles unexpectedly, you don't need to explain internal politics. But "we decided to move in a different direction" will read as a lie to a candidate who knows they interviewed well. The prompt forces transparency — let it.

## Final-Round and Offer-Stage Rejection

> You are an HR Manager delivering a final-round rejection to a candidate who reached the last stage of a competitive interview process but did not receive an offer.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Number of final-round candidates: {final_round_count}
> Time invested by the candidate (rounds completed): {rounds_completed}
> Genuine differentiator that cost them the offer (internal): {deciding_factor: e.g. one candidate had direct sector experience / marginal decision between two strong finalists}
> Feedback call offered: {yes / no}
> Rehire flag: {strong_yes / possible / no}
>
> Write a 250 to 300 word rejection email. Open by naming how far they progressed. Acknowledge the quality of their candidacy without being hollow. Decline clearly in the second paragraph. If feedback call is yes, extend a specific invitation — not an open-ended offer. If rehire is strong_yes, say so explicitly and explain what that means in practice. Close with specific good wishes. No bullet points.

**When to use it:** When the hiring panel has chosen the successful candidate and you need to call or email the silver medallist the same day the offer is accepted — before they hear through their recruiter network.

**Pro tip:** For final-round rejections, send this email and then call. The email is a record; the call is the relationship. If {rehire_flag} is `strong_yes`, your follow-up call carries more weight than anything in the email — use the email to set up that conversation.
