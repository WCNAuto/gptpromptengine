## After the Application Screen: High-Volume Rejection Emails

> You are an HR Manager writing a candidate rejection email after an initial application review.
>
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Reason for rejection (internal, do not include verbatim): {rejection_reason — e.g. "missing required certification", "salary expectation mismatch", "not enough years of experience"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {warm_and_brief / professional_and_neutral}
> Encourage future applications: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email. Open by thanking the candidate for applying. Do not state the specific rejection reason. Close with a single sentence either inviting or not inviting future applications based on the variable above. No hollow phrases like "we were blown away by the calibre of applicants". Sign off from the HR team, not a named individual.

**When to use it:** Monday morning after the application window closes and you have 40+ declines to process before your 10am hiring manager sync.

**Pro tip:** If {encourage_future_applications} is "no" because the candidate was flagged as a poor culture fit or had a conduct note, run that output through your legal team's approved language list before sending. ChatGPT will write something warm that may inadvertently invite re-application you don't want.

> You are an HR Manager sending a post-application rejection to a candidate who applied through a job board and has not yet been contacted by the company.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Department: {department}
> Days since application submitted: {days_since_application}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Talent pool opt-in option available: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 90 to 120 word rejection email. Acknowledge the delay if {days_since_application} is more than 14. Keep the tone professional and respectful. If {talent_pool_opt_in} is "yes", add one sentence at the end inviting them to opt into future role alerts. Do not use passive voice constructions like "it has been decided". Write in plain, direct English.

**When to use it:** When you're clearing a backlog of applications from a role that closed three weeks ago and candidates have been waiting without a response.

**Pro tip:** If it has been more than 30 days, add the acknowledgment of delay as your first sentence, not buried at the end. Candidates who have waited that long notice when you gloss over it, and it damages your Glassdoor score more than the rejection itself.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate who applied for an entry-level role and is clearly underqualified but showed initiative in their cover letter.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Something specific they did well in their application: {specific_positive — e.g. "tailored cover letter referencing our sustainability report", "included a portfolio link"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {encouraging / neutral}
> Suggest they apply again in future: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 110 to 140 word rejection email. Acknowledge the specific positive thing they did. Do not offer false hope about their qualifications. If {suggest_reapply} is "yes", name a concrete condition such as gaining a year of experience rather than a vague "keep an eye on our website". Keep the tone human without being patronising.

**When to use it:** When a recent graduate or career changer sends a genuinely good application for a role they're not ready for yet, and you want to leave the door open without misleading them.

**Pro tip:** Don't paste the candidate's cover letter into ChatGPT to pull out the specific positive — that's a GDPR risk if you're using a non-enterprise AI tier. Write the {specific_positive} variable yourself from memory or your ATS notes.

> You are an HR Manager sending a bulk rejection to candidates who applied for a role that has been cancelled or put on hold before interviews began.
>
> Role that was cancelled: {job_title}
> Reason for cancellation (internal — do not include): {internal_reason — e.g. "headcount freeze", "restructure", "budget cut"}
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Will the role be re-advertised later: {yes / no / unknown}
> Tone: {apologetic / straightforward}
>
> Write a 100 to 120 word email informing the candidate that the role will not be moving to interview stage. Do not reveal the internal reason. If {role_readvertised} is "yes", mention that the role may be re-advertised and they are welcome to reapply. If "no" or "unknown", do not speculate. Open by apologising for the inconvenience. Do not say "due to circumstances beyond our control" — be direct that a business decision was made.

**When to use it:** When a headcount freeze hits mid-recruitment and you have a shortlist of 12 candidates who submitted applications in good faith.

**Pro tip:** If the role is likely to come back under a different job title after a restructure, do not say "this role" in the email. Say "this specific position" so you're not contradicting yourself when the same job re-appears in six weeks.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who applied for an internal transfer and was not selected after the posting closed.
>
> Candidate full name: {candidate_full_name}
> Current role: {current_role}
> Role they applied for internally: {target_role}
> Hiring manager name: {hiring_manager_name}
> Reason for non-selection (internal — do not state directly): {rejection_reason — e.g. "stronger internal candidate selected", "team preferred external hire", "skill gap in X area"}
> Next step for the candidate's development: {suggested_next_step — e.g. "L&D programme", "stretch project", "revisit in 6 months"}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word internal rejection email. Acknowledge that applying internally takes courage. Do not reveal that another internal candidate was chosen if that is the reason. Offer the {suggested_next_step} as a concrete path forward. Set a specific time frame of 3 to 6 months for a follow-up conversation. Tone: direct but supportive. This person still works here and you will see them on Monday.

**When to use it:** After an internal posting closes and you need to tell a current employee they didn't get the role before the successful candidate's announcement goes out.

**Pro tip:** Send this before the successful candidate's appointment is announced internally. If the rejected internal candidate hears via the company Slack channel before they hear from you, the damage to trust takes months to repair.

## After the Phone Screen or Recruiter Call

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate after a phone screen conducted by a recruiter.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Recruiter who conducted the screen: {recruiter_name}
> Specific reason the candidate did not progress (internal — do not state directly): {rejection_reason — e.g. "salary expectation $30k above band", "availability doesn't match shift pattern", "communication skills below threshold for client-facing role"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Time since the phone screen: {days_since_screen}
>
> Write a 110 to 130 word rejection email from {recruiter_name}. Thank the candidate for their time on the call. Do not reference the specific rejection reason. If {days_since_screen} is more than 5, acknowledge the delay. Close with a genuine wish for their search, not a hollow "we wish you all the best in your future endeavours". Sign off personally from {recruiter_name}.

**When to use it:** Friday afternoon when your recruiter has three screens from Tuesday still sitting in their drafts folder because they've been buried in interviews.

**Pro tip:** A personally signed rejection from the recruiter who actually spoke to the candidate converts much better on Glassdoor sentiment than an unsigned "HR Team" email. It costs nothing to personalise the sign-off.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who passed the phone screen but scored below the cut-off on a pre-employment assessment.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Assessment type: {assessment_type — e.g. "numerical reasoning test", "coding challenge", "situational judgement questionnaire"}
> Score outcome: {below_threshold / borderline}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Offer to share feedback: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 120 to 150 word rejection email. Acknowledge that the candidate invested time in completing the assessment. Do not share the score or the threshold. If {offer_feedback} is "yes", offer a brief feedback call and include a one-sentence instruction on how to request it. If "no", do not raise the subject of feedback. Tone: professional and matter-of-fact. Do not over-apologise.

**When to use it:** When your ATS auto-scores assessments and triggers a batch of declines you need to review and send before candidates follow up asking for results.

**Pro tip:** If {offer_feedback} is "yes", include the feedback call offer only for roles above a certain seniority. For high-volume entry-level roles where you're rejecting 200 candidates on a cut-off score, offering feedback to all of them creates a call load that will consume your entire week.

> You are an HR Manager writing a post-phone-screen rejection to a candidate who was enthusiastic and well-qualified, but you are proceeding with a stronger match.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> One genuine strength from the call: {genuine_strength — e.g. "clear understanding of enterprise sales cycles", "strong track record in regulated environments"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Keep in talent pool: {yes / no}
> Tone: {warm / professional}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word rejection email. Open by naming the {genuine_strength} — this must be specific, not generic praise. Explain that you are moving forward with a candidate whose experience is a closer match for the role at this time. Do not say "we had an exceptionally strong candidate pool". If {keep_in_talent_pool} is "yes", specify what kind of roles you would reach out for and give a time horizon of no more than 6 months. Do not make promises you cannot keep.

**When to use it:** When you're rejecting a candidate who was genuinely good but came second, and you actually do want to keep them warm for a future role.

**Pro tip:** Only use the talent pool language if you have a functioning talent pool with a re-contact process. If your ATS talent pool is a folder no one opens, either fix that before using this prompt or set {keep_in_talent_pool} to "no".

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email after a phone screen where the candidate disclosed a salary expectation significantly outside your budget.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Candidate's stated salary expectation: {candidate_salary_expectation}
> Your approved salary band (do not share exact figure): {your_band — e.g. "mid-$70k range", "£45k to £52k"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Is the role advertised with a salary: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 100 to 120 word rejection email. Be transparent that the compensation expectations are not aligned — do not pretend it's a skills issue. If {role_advertised_with_salary} is "no", acknowledge that clearer upfront communication about the band would have saved both parties time. Keep the tone respectful and frank. Do not suggest the candidate lower their expectations. Do not apologise excessively.

**When to use it:** When a candidate has interviewed well but named a number 40% above your band, and you want to close the loop without wasting a hiring manager's time.

**Pro tip:** If your company repeatedly hits this problem, use the output of this prompt as evidence in your next conversation with your Talent Acquisition lead about the case for publishing salary bands. The pattern becomes obvious when you're writing the same email fifty times.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who requested a phone screen for a role that has already been filled, but whose profile is relevant to future openings.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role they expressed interest in: {job_title}
> Date role was filled: {date_role_filled}
> Relevant future role type: {future_role_type — e.g. "mid-level data analyst roles", "operations coordinator positions"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Opt-in to job alerts available: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 110 to 130 word email informing the candidate that the role has been filled. Thank them for proactively reaching out. Note that their background is relevant for {future_role_type}. If {opt_in_available} is "yes", give a one-sentence instruction on how to set up job alerts. Keep the tone direct and warm. Do not use "unfortunately at this time" — write in plain English.

**When to use it:** When a speculative enquiry or LinkedIn message lands in your inbox for a role you closed last month.

**Pro tip:** Candidates who reach out proactively are often your best future hires. Flag this person in your ATS with a note before you send the email, so you have a record when the next similar role opens.

## After the First-Round Interview

> You are an HR Manager writing a post-first-round interview rejection email on behalf of a hiring manager.
>
> Candidate first name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Hiring manager name: {hiring_manager_name}
> One specific area where the candidate did not meet the bar: {gap_area — e.g. "experience managing cross-functional stakeholders", "depth of technical knowledge in cloud infrastructure"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Offer feedback call: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word rejection email. Thank the candidate for attending the interview and preparing for it. Name the {gap_area} in plain language — frame it as a requirement for this specific role, not a flaw in the candidate. If {offer_feedback} is "yes", invite them to request a 15-minute debrief call with specific instructions for how to do so. Close with a genuine note — not a template phrase. Send from {hiring_manager_name}, not HR.

**When to use it:** When the hiring manager debriefs you on Wednesday and you need to get the decline out before the candidate follows up on Thursday.

**Pro tip:** Sending the rejection from the hiring manager's name rather than HR significantly reduces the likelihood of a candidate disputing the decision or escalating. It signals that the decision was substantive, not administrative.
