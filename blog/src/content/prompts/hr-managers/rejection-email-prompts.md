## Post-Application Rejection (Before Interview)

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate who applied for an open role but did not meet the minimum requirements for screening.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Primary reason for rejection (internal use, do not quote verbatim): {rejection_reason}
> Tone: {warm_and_brief / professional_and_neutral}
> Time since application submitted: {days_since_application}
>
> Write a 120 to 150 word rejection email. Open by thanking them for applying. Acknowledge the volume of applications without using it as an excuse. State clearly they will not be moving forward. Do not explain the rejection reason in detail. Close with a genuine wish for their search. Use plain language. No legal caveats, no vague "we'll keep your CV on file" unless the company genuinely does.

**When to use it:** Monday morning when you have 40 unscreened applications to clear before the hiring manager asks for a shortlist by noon.

**Pro tip:** If {days_since_application} is over 14 days, tell ChatGPT to add one sentence acknowledging the wait — something like "We appreciate your patience while we worked through a high volume of applications." Candidates who waited two weeks notice when that line is missing and it affects employer brand.

> You are an HR Manager sending a bulk rejection email to multiple candidates who applied but were screened out at the CV review stage for a technical role.
>
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Missing qualification that disqualified most candidates: {missing_qualification}
> Number of candidates in this batch: {batch_size}
> Tone: {professional / conversational}
> Hiring timeline for future roles: {actively_hiring / not_currently_hiring}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email written for a single named recipient but structured so it reads naturally even when sent to many people. Do not use "Dear Candidate." Open with the role name and the decision. In the middle, mention one specific quality the company looks for in future applicants without implying this candidate was close. Close with a clear statement on whether they should apply again and when.

**When to use it:** When you are bulk-rejecting a cohort of candidates who all missed the same hard requirement — for example, a missing certification or visa status — and you want something faster than writing 30 individual emails but more human than a one-liner.

**Pro tip:** If {missing_qualification} is something candidates could obtain (e.g. a specific licence), add a note in the variables telling ChatGPT to include one sentence encouraging them to reapply once they have it. That converts a dead-end rejection into a pipeline touch.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate who applied speculatively without a live vacancy, via a general interest or "work with us" submission.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Area they expressed interest in: {functional_area}
> Realistic likelihood of a role opening in the next 6 months: {likely / unlikely / unknown}
> Tone: {encouraging / neutral}
> One genuine strength from their application (optional, leave blank if not reviewed): {standout_strength}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word email declining to progress their speculative application. Be honest about whether a relevant role is likely. If {standout_strength} is provided, reference it specifically — do not use generic praise. If {realistic_likelihood} is "unlikely", say so clearly rather than leaving them waiting. Close with one concrete action they can take, such as following the company on LinkedIn or applying to a named live role if one exists.

**When to use it:** When your careers inbox has piled up with speculative CVs and you want to clear them without leaving candidates in limbo for six months wondering if they are still being considered.

**Pro tip:** Avoid telling ChatGPT to say "we'll reach out if something comes up" unless you have a system to actually do that. Candidates remember those promises and it erodes trust when no one contacts them. If you have no talent database process, tell ChatGPT explicitly: "Do not promise future outreach."

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate who applied for a senior-level role but whose experience is clearly more junior than required.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Seniority level of the role: {seniority_level}
> Years of experience the candidate appears to have: {candidate_experience_years}
> Years of experience required: {required_experience_years}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word rejection email. Do not condescend. Do not say they are "not quite there yet" unless the tone is genuinely encouraging. Acknowledge their initiative in applying for a stretch role. State the mismatch clearly without going into a list of missing requirements. If a more junior version of the role exists at the company, mention it in one sentence. Close with specific encouragement — not a platitude.

**When to use it:** When a recent graduate or mid-level candidate has applied for a director or head-of role and you need to decline without making them feel foolish for reaching.

**Pro tip:** Tell ChatGPT the candidate's actual career stage in {candidate_experience_years} rather than leaving it vague. "3 years of experience applying for a role requiring 10" produces a more calibrated email than generic seniority-gap language.

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate whose application was strong on paper but who applied for a role that has since been put on hold or cancelled internally.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role they applied for: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Reason role is on hold (internal — do not state directly): {internal_reason}
> Likelihood the role reopens: {likely_within_3_months / unlikely / unknown}
> Tone: {apologetic / straightforward}
>
> Write a 130 to 150 word email explaining the role is no longer moving forward due to internal business reasons, without disclosing {internal_reason}. Be honest that the decision is unrelated to their candidacy. If {likelihood_role_reopens} is "likely_within_3_months", say you may be in touch and give a rough timeframe. If "unlikely" or "unknown", do not make that promise. The email should feel like a genuine communication, not a legal disclaimer dressed up as an apology.

**When to use it:** When a hiring freeze, restructure, or budget cut means you have to reject candidates who did nothing wrong and who may already have invested time in the process.

**Pro tip:** If the role was cancelled after the candidate submitted documents or completed a task, add a variable like {effort_candidate_invested} and tell ChatGPT to acknowledge it specifically. Skipping this when someone spent three hours on a task damages employer brand more than the rejection itself.

## After Phone Screen or First Interview

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate following a phone screen where they did not demonstrate the level of experience needed to move to the interview stage.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Duration of phone screen: {screen_duration_minutes} minutes
> Main gap identified during the screen: {primary_gap}
> Interviewer name (optional): {interviewer_name}
> Tone: {direct / warm}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word rejection email. Thank them for their time and reference the phone screen specifically — not a generic "interview." Name the {primary_gap} in a way that is honest but not harsh (e.g. "we were looking for deeper hands-on experience in X" rather than "you didn't have X"). Do not invite them to reapply unless the gap is genuinely closable. Close with one sentence that is specific to their situation, not a boilerplate good luck line.

**When to use it:** Friday afternoon when your phone screen notes are still fresh and you want to close the loop before the weekend so candidates aren't left waiting over two days.

**Pro tip:** If {primary_gap} is something sensitive — for example, a communication style issue that showed up on the call — do not put it in the email. Tell ChatGPT to use a neutral phrase like "alignment with the level of the role" instead. Specific feedback on soft skills in a rejection email creates legal exposure and rarely helps the candidate.

> You are an HR Manager sending a post-phone-screen rejection email to a candidate who was technically qualified but whose salary expectations came in significantly above the approved budget.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Approved salary range: {approved_salary_range}
> Candidate's stated expectation: {candidate_salary_expectation}
> Whether the range could flex in future: {yes / no / unknown}
> Tone: {honest / diplomatic}
>
> Write a 140 to 160 word email that is honest about the reason — salary misalignment — without making the candidate feel their expectations are unreasonable. Do not apologise excessively. State that you will not be moving forward, explain the reason clearly in one sentence, and — only if {range_could_flex} is "yes" — leave the door open for a future conversation. Do not ask them to reconsider their expectations. Close professionally.

**When to use it:** When a strong candidate's salary expectation is 20% above your band and you'd rather be upfront than waste their time in two more interview rounds before it becomes obvious.

**Pro tip:** Do not include the {approved_salary_range} in the email unless your company publicly advertises it. Some compensation strategies are confidential. Tell ChatGPT to reference "our approved budget for this role" instead of the specific figure.

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email after a first structured interview where the candidate did not score above the minimum threshold on the agreed scoring criteria.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Interview format: {competency_based / technical / panel}
> Number of interviewers: {interviewer_count}
> General area where the candidate fell short (do not quote scores): {shortfall_area}
> Tone: {professional / empathetic}
>
> Write a 160 to 200 word rejection email. Open by thanking them for coming in and naming the interview format so it feels specific. State the decision clearly in the first paragraph. In the second paragraph, acknowledge one genuine strength observed without overpromising. Reference the {shortfall_area} in general terms — not as a criticism but as context. Close by wishing them well in their search. Do not offer to "provide feedback" unless {tone} is empathetic and you are prepared to follow through.

**When to use it:** When you have a structured scoring process and a candidate fell below threshold across most competencies — you need to explain the outcome without disclosing internal scores.

**Pro tip:** The phrase "we had many strong candidates" is almost always seen as hollow. Tell ChatGPT explicitly not to use it. If competition was genuinely fierce, specify the actual number of applicants in your notes and let ChatGPT reference volume without the cliché.

> You are an HR Manager sending a rejection email to a candidate who interviewed well but whose role expectations — scope, autonomy, team size — do not match what the company can offer at this time.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Expectation mismatch area: {mismatch_area}
> How the mismatch was identified: {how_identified: interview_discussion / reference_check / written_answers}
> Tone: {candid / warm}
> Whether a better-fit role may open in future: {yes / no}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word rejection email that frames the decision as a fit issue, not a capability issue. Be specific about the type of mismatch — do not hide behind "we've decided to move in a different direction." If {better_fit_role_future} is "yes", be concrete: say what type of role and roughly when. If "no", close cleanly without false hope.

**When to use it:** When a candidate is genuinely strong but wants to lead a function when the role is individual contributor, or expects a larger team than you have — a mismatch that will cause problems on both sides if ignored.

**Pro tip:** If {how_identified} is "interview_discussion," the candidate already knows the mismatch exists and your email should acknowledge the conversation directly. If it was identified through reference check or written answers, keep the reference vague so you don't disclose your assessment method.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email for a candidate who completed a take-home technical assessment or work sample and whose submission did not meet the required standard.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Type of task completed: {task_type}
> Approximate time the task was expected to take: {expected_task_hours} hours
> Specific area where the submission fell short: {shortfall_area}
> Tone: {respectful / professional}
>
> Write a 170 to 210 word rejection email. Open by explicitly acknowledging the time and effort the candidate invested in {task_type}. State the decision clearly. Provide one specific piece of feedback about {shortfall_area} — not a generic "it didn't meet our standard" — that the candidate could actually use. Do not list every issue with the submission. Close by thanking them again for the effort and wishing them well.

**When to use it:** After a take-home task round where you asked candidates to invest 3 to 6 hours of work and you owe them more than a one-line decline.

**Pro tip:** If your legal team or manager has told you not to give specific feedback in writing, replace the {shortfall_area} detail with a general statement and move the real feedback to an optional phone call offer. You can tell ChatGPT: "Offer a 10-minute debrief call instead of written feedback details."

## Final-Round and Near-Miss Rejections

>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Number of final-round candidates: {final_round_candidate_count}
> What made the chosen candidate stronger (internal — do not quote directly): {differentiating_factor}

> Tone: {genuinely_warm / professional}
>

If {keep_warm} is "yes", include a specific and honest invitation to stay connected — not a vague "we'll keep you in mind." Close with a warm and professional sign-off.
