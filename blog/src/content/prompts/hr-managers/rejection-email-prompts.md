## Post-Application Rejections (No Interview Offered)

### Prompt 1 — High-volume applicant who didn't meet minimum qualifications

> You are an HR Manager sending a post-application rejection email to a candidate who applied for an open role and did not meet the minimum qualifications. No interview was conducted.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Department: {department}
> Reason for rejection (internal, do not quote verbatim): {reason_e.g._missing_required_certification_or_years_experience}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {warm_and_brief / neutral_and_professional / formal}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email. Open by thanking the candidate for applying. In one sentence, state the role has been filled or progressed with other applicants — do not enumerate the reason. Close with a genuine but non-committal encouragement to watch for future openings. Do not use phrases like "We were overwhelmed with applications." Do not invite the candidate to reapply unless the tone variable is set to warm_and_brief, in which case include one sentence inviting them to apply for future roles.

**When to use it:** Monday morning after the application window closes and you need to clear 40 to 80 unqualified applicants before the week's interview slate starts.

**Pro tip:** If your ATS auto-populates candidate names with inconsistent capitalisation (e.g. "JOHN" or "john"), set a note to paste the name manually — ChatGPT will reproduce whatever you give it verbatim, so garbage in is garbage out.

### Prompt 2 — Passive candidate sourced by recruiter who isn't a fit after CV review

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate your team proactively sourced via LinkedIn or a job board. After reviewing their CV, you've decided not to progress them. They have not applied; your team reached out first.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role you approached them for: {job_title}
> Reason not proceeding (internal only): {reason_e.g._compensation_expectations_too_high_or_skills_gap_in_X}
> Recruiter or sourcer who contacted them: {recruiter_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {apologetic_and_warm / professional_and_direct}
>
> Write a 120 to 150 word email. Acknowledge that your team approached them and that their time reviewing the opportunity is appreciated. State you will not be moving forward without naming the specific reason. If tone is apologetic_and_warm, include one sentence expressing regret for any inconvenience caused. Close by leaving the door open to future contact. Do not suggest the candidate apply via the careers page — that contradicts the sourced relationship.

**When to use it:** After a sourcing sprint where your team pinged 30 candidates and only 6 are worth progressing — you owe the other 24 a respectful close-out because you approached them.

**Pro tip:** Sourced candidates who receive no response after being approached are the most likely to post a negative Glassdoor review. Even a 120-word email cuts that risk significantly. Use this prompt the same day the decision is made, not at end of week.

### Prompt 3 — Internal employee who applied for a posted role and wasn't shortlisted

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to an internal employee who applied for an internal job posting and was not shortlisted for interview. Their direct manager does not yet know they applied.
>
> Employee name: {employee_first_name}
> Their current role: {current_job_title}
> Role they applied for: {target_job_title}
> Their tenure at the company: {tenure_e.g._2_years_4_months}
> Whether you want them to speak with their manager: {yes_encourage / no_keep_confidential}
> Tone: {supportive_and_direct / neutral_and_formal}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word internal rejection email. Acknowledge the application respectfully. State they were not selected for interview without specifying which other candidates were. If the variable is yes_encourage, include one sentence suggesting a conversation with their manager about development goals. If no_keep_confidential, omit any mention of their manager. Close by affirming their value as an employee. Do not use phrases like "we had stronger candidates" or any comparison language.

**When to use it:** When an internal job board post closes and you have employees in the rejected pile whose managers are unaware — this is a common situation in companies with self-service internal applications.

**Pro tip:** Internal rejections have legal sensitivity around protected characteristics. Before sending, confirm the candidate's protected characteristic profile is not disproportionately represented in your rejection pile for this role. If it is, escalate before sending any emails.

### Prompt 4 — Candidate who applied for a role that has been put on hold or cancelled

> You are an HR Manager emailing a candidate who applied for a role that has since been put on hold or cancelled by the business before any interviews took place. The candidate submitted a complete application.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Department: {department}
> Reason role is on hold or cancelled (internal only): {reason_e.g._budget_freeze_or_restructure}
> Whether the role may reopen: {likely / unlikely / unknown}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word email. Be honest that the role is no longer moving forward at this time without naming the internal business reason. If the role is likely to reopen, include one sentence inviting them to look out for the re-posting. If unlikely or unknown, do not raise the prospect of reposting. Thank them for the effort of applying. Do not apologise excessively or use passive corporate language like "we regret to inform you at this time that circumstances have evolved."

**When to use it:** Immediately after a hiring freeze is communicated internally — before candidates spend more time following up on a pipeline that no longer exists.

**Pro tip:** If the freeze affects multiple roles and you are sending this to 50+ candidates at once, prepend the prompt with "Do not include any role-specific details that would make bulk sending obvious." This prevents AI from adding detail that contradicts your blind-send.

### Prompt 5 — Candidate who submitted a speculative application with no open role

> You are an HR Manager replying to a candidate who submitted a speculative or unsolicited application — there is no open role they applied to, and there is nothing currently in the pipeline that matches their profile.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Their self-described area of expertise: {candidate_field_e.g._supply_chain_analytics}
> Whether their CV is worth keeping on file: {yes_keep_on_file / no_no_match}
> How they submitted: {email_to_careers_inbox / LinkedIn_message / website_contact_form}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 110 to 140 word reply. Acknowledge the initiative of reaching out directly. State that there are no current openings matching their profile. If yes_keep_on_file, include one sentence confirming you are retaining their details and will make contact if a relevant role arises — include a note that this is not a guarantee. If no_no_match, do not suggest their CV will be retained. Close warmly regardless of outcome.

**When to use it:** Friday afternoon when the careers inbox has a backlog of speculative CVs that have been sitting unread for two weeks — clear them in one batch.

**Pro tip:** "We'll keep your CV on file" creates an expectation and, in some jurisdictions, a GDPR data-retention obligation. Only set the variable to yes_keep_on_file if your ATS actually has a talent pool function that is actively used. Otherwise set it to no_no_match every time.

## Post-First-Interview Rejections

### Prompt 6 — Candidate rejected after a screening call with a recruiter

> You are an HR Manager or recruiter writing a rejection email to a candidate who completed a 20 to 30 minute phone or video screening call but will not be progressed to the next stage.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Main reason not progressing (internal only): {reason_e.g._salary_expectation_mismatch_or_communication_concerns}
> Whether feedback was promised during the call: {yes_feedback_promised / no_feedback_not_promised}
> One piece of brief, constructive, shareable feedback (if applicable): {feedback_e.g._stronger_examples_of_stakeholder_management_would_help}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 140 to 180 word rejection email. Thank the candidate for their time. State you will not be moving forward. If feedback was promised, include the one-sentence piece of constructive feedback provided. If no feedback was promised, omit any feedback entirely and do not hint at the internal reason. Do not use phrases like "you were not the right fit" or "we had stronger candidates." Close with a genuine wish for their search.

**When to use it:** The day after a block of screening calls, while the conversations are still fresh and before candidates start following up.

**Pro tip:** If you promised feedback on the call but now can't recall specifics, do not invent generic feedback — it reads as hollow and can generate a reply asking for more detail. It is better to say "I'm not in a position to share specific feedback by email" than to paste something vague.

### Prompt 7 — Candidate rejected after a first-stage panel interview, strong performer but role filled

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who performed well in a first-stage panel interview but will not progress because the role has been filled by another candidate or internal hire.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> One genuine strength observed in the interview: {observed_strength_e.g._excellent_data_storytelling_skills}
> Whether you want to add them to a talent pool for future roles: {yes / no}
> Timeframe before a similar role may open: {estimated_timeframe_or_unknown}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 160 to 200 word rejection email. Open by naming the specific strength observed — this must feel personal, not boilerplate. Explain the role has been filled. If yes talent pool, invite them to future roles and give the rough timeframe if known. If no, close without raising future opportunities. Do not apologise repeatedly. Do not use phrases like "this was one of the hardest decisions" unless you have direct evidence the hiring manager said so.

**When to use it:** When a strong second-place candidate deserves a better email than a form rejection — especially in niche technical or senior roles where employer brand with this person matters.

**Pro tip:** Naming one real, specific strength (e.g. "the way you walked through the P&L rebuild in Q2" rather than "your analytical skills") is the single biggest differentiator between a rejection that preserves goodwill and one that feels automated. If you can't name something specific, use Prompt 6 instead.

### Prompt 8 — Candidate rejected after first interview due to misalignment on remote or hybrid expectations

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate after a first-stage interview where it became clear there is a fundamental misalignment on working location or remote/hybrid expectations.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Your company's stated working arrangement for this role: {arrangement_e.g._3_days_in_office_London}
> Candidate's stated preference: {candidate_preference_e.g._fully_remote}
> Whether this was disclosed in the job ad: {yes_it_was_in_the_ad / no_it_was_not_clearly_stated}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word rejection email. Frame the decision around practical fit rather than any suggestion the candidate's preference is wrong. If the working arrangement was not clearly stated in the ad, include one brief sentence acknowledging that the role description could have been clearer. Do not apologise more than once. Do not suggest they are wrong to want remote work. Close with a genuine wish for their search.

**When to use it:** When a candidate only disclosed their location preferences during the interview and you need to reject without creating a poor candidate experience around a logistical issue neither party should be embarrassed about.

**Pro tip:** If the working arrangement was not in the job ad, acknowledge it in the email — don't bury it. Candidates who feel misled are more likely to leave negative reviews on Glassdoor or Indeed. A single honest sentence costs you nothing and prevents most of that.

### Prompt 9 — Candidate rejected after first interview because the role was re-levelled downward

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate after a first-stage interview because the hiring manager has since re-levelled the role — the seniority or scope changed after the interview took place, making the candidate overqualified.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_first_name}
> Original job title as advertised: {original_job_title}
> New level or revised scope: {new_level_e.g._coordinator_instead_of_manager}
> Whether the re-levelled role will be re-advertised: {yes_reposting / no_filling_internally / undecided}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {transparent_and_direct / professionally_brief}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word rejection email. Be transparent that the role scope changed after the interview — this is the honest reason and the candidate deserves to hear it. Do not frame it as a performance issue. If the role is being re-advertised at the new level, mention it only if the candidate might be interested in applying at that level. If filling internally or undecided, do not raise it. Close by affirming their experience was appropriate for the original brief.

**When to use it:** When a hiring manager changes their mind on seniority mid-process and you are left explaining a rejection that has nothing to do with the candidate's performance.

**Pro tip:** Candidates rejected due to re-levelling are uniquely primed to refer peers who fit the new level — if your company has a referral scheme, this is one of the few rejection emails where mentioning it is contextually appropriate and not tone-deaf.
