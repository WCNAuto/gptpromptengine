## Post-Interview Rejection Emails

> You are an HR manager writing a post-interview rejection email to a candidate.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Interview stage reached: {interview_stage: phone screen / first interview / second interview / final round}
> Primary reason for rejection: {rejection_reason: skills gap / stronger candidate selected / culture fit / role cancelled}
> One genuine positive from the interview: {interview_highlight}
> Tone: {tone: warm / neutral / formal}
>
> Write a 150 to 200 word rejection email. Open by thanking them for their time at the {interview_stage}. Name the {interview_highlight} specifically in the second sentence. State the decision clearly without over-explaining. Do not promise future opportunities unless instructed. Close with a single sentence of goodwill. No bullet points.

**When to use it:** The morning after a final-round decision when you have four or five rejections to send before the offer letter goes out.

**Pro tip:** If the role was cancelled rather than filled by someone else, change {rejection_reason} to "role cancelled" — the AI will avoid phrases like "a stronger candidate" which read as hollow and untrue in that context.

> You are an HR manager sending a rejection email after a second interview for a senior leadership role.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Hiring manager who interviewed them: {hiring_manager_name}
> Specific strength the candidate showed: {candidate_strength}
> Reason they were not selected (internal use only, inform tone): {internal_reason: narrower domain experience / compensation misalignment / another finalist had direct industry experience}
> Notice period at current employer: {candidate_notice_period}
> Tone: {tone: respectful / warm / direct}
>
> Write a 200 to 250 word rejection email from the HR manager, referencing {hiring_manager_name} as having personally appreciated the conversation. Acknowledge {candidate_strength} in one sentence. Do not disclose {internal_reason}. Close by inviting the candidate to stay in touch on LinkedIn with a single low-pressure sentence. No generic phrases like "we were impressed by all candidates."

**When to use it:** When a candidate reached the final two and the hiring manager wants the rejection to reflect the time the candidate invested.

**Pro tip:** Senior candidates talk. If the {internal_reason} is compensation misalignment, never hint at it in the email — it opens a negotiation the role has already been closed on.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email after a phone screen.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Department: {department}
> Core requirement the candidate did not meet: {unmet_requirement: years of experience / required certification / location / right-to-work status}
> Time from application to rejection: {days_since_application} days
> Tone: {tone: brief / polite / neutral}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email. Do not over-explain. Acknowledge they took time for the call. State the decision in one sentence, referencing that {unmet_requirement} was a hard requirement for the role. Do not invite them to reapply unless {unmet_requirement} is something they can address in the future (e.g. gaining a certification). Keep the sign-off professional but not effusive.

**When to use it:** When you have 20 or more phone-screen rejections to process in one sitting and you need a fast, consistent email that does not expose the company to a discrimination complaint.

**Pro tip:** Referencing a specific unmet requirement rather than "we have decided to move forward with other candidates" significantly reduces reply emails asking why they were rejected. The AI will do this automatically if you fill in {unmet_requirement} accurately.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who interviewed well but whose salary expectation was above budget.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Salary expectation stated by candidate: {candidate_salary_expectation}
> Budget ceiling for the role: {role_budget_ceiling}
> Number of interview rounds completed: {interview_rounds_completed}
> Tone: {tone: transparent / warm / neutral}
>
> Write a 180 to 220 word rejection email. Do not reveal the exact {role_budget_ceiling}. Acknowledge the candidate's time across {interview_rounds_completed} rounds. State that the role's compensation structure and the candidate's expectations were not aligned, and that this is a business constraint rather than a reflection of their skills or interview performance. Do not suggest they lower their expectations. Close with a genuine invitation to be considered for future roles at the appropriate level.

**When to use it:** When you genuinely liked a candidate but the budget conversation collapsed at offer stage — this email preserves the relationship without making promises you cannot keep.

**Pro tip:** Do not let the AI write "we hope to work together in the future" unless you mean it. Change {tone} to "transparent" and the output will be warmer but more honest — it will avoid hollow future-promise language automatically.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to an internal candidate who applied for a promotion and was not selected.
>
> Employee name: {employee_name}
> Current role: {current_role}
> Role they applied for: {applied_role}
> Who got the role (internal or external): {successful_hire_type: internal colleague / external hire}
> Key development area the employee should focus on: {development_focus}
> Manager who will follow up in person: {managers_name}
> Tone: {tone: direct / supportive / candid}
>
> Write a 200 to 250 word email. Acknowledge the courage it takes to apply internally. State the outcome clearly in the first paragraph. Do not compare the employee to the successful candidate. Name {development_focus} as a concrete next step, not a criticism. Reference that {managers_name} will follow up with a development conversation within the week. Do not use the phrase "we encourage you to continue growing."

**When to use it:** Before an all-hands or team meeting where the successful hire will be announced — this email should land in the internal candidate's inbox first.

**Pro tip:** If {successful_hire_type} is "external hire," the AI will soften the language around that fact without you asking — but review the output carefully to ensure it is not evasive. Internal candidates who find out via the company announcement that an external person was hired feel doubly dismissed.

## High-Volume Application Rejection Emails

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email for candidates who applied but were not shortlisted for interview.
>
> Role title: {job_title}
> Number of applications received: {application_count}
> Stage the candidate reached: {stage: applied / CV reviewed}
> One honest line about competition for the role: {competition_context: e.g. "we received over 300 applications for two positions"}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Tone: {tone: brief / respectful / neutral}
>
> Write a 90 to 120 word rejection email suitable for bulk sending. Do not use "Dear Candidate" — use "Hi [first name]" with a note that the recruiter should mail-merge the name. Acknowledge the application briefly. Include {competition_context} to give the rejection context. Do not say "we were impressed by your application" if the CV was not reviewed in depth. Close with one sentence pointing to future roles at {company_name}.

**When to use it:** End of a busy hiring sprint when you need to clear 200 pending application statuses before the weekend.

**Pro tip:** Insert a note in the output reminding yourself to mail-merge {candidate_name} before sending — ask the AI to add "MAIL MERGE NAME BEFORE SENDING" in brackets at the top of the email. It will do it if you add that instruction to the prompt.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email for candidates who completed an online skills assessment but did not reach the interview stage.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Assessment type: {assessment_type: coding test / psychometric / case study / writing task}
> Approximate time the assessment takes: {assessment_duration_minutes} minutes
> What the assessment measured (general): {assessment_focus}
> Tone: {tone: appreciative / neutral / brief}
>
> Write a 130 to 160 word rejection email. Open by thanking the candidate specifically for completing the {assessment_type}, acknowledging it took approximately {assessment_duration_minutes} minutes of their time. State the decision clearly. Do not share scores or ranking. Do not give feedback on their performance unless instructed to do so. Close with a note that their details will be retained for {retention_period} months in line with data policy.

**When to use it:** After a batch assessment closes and you are processing all non-progressing candidates at once before the next stage interviews begin.

**Pro tip:** Acknowledging the time a candidate spent on an assessment reduces negative Glassdoor reviews of your hiring process. The {assessment_duration_minutes} variable does the work here — do not skip it.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to candidates who applied via a job board but whose applications were automatically screened out.
>
> Role: {job_title}
> Job board used: {job_board: LinkedIn / Indeed / Reed / Seek / other}
> Screening criterion that filtered them out: {screening_criterion: location / salary range / experience level / visa requirement}
> Company name: {company_name}
> Response time since application: {days_elapsed} days
> Tone: {tone: neutral / brief / polite}
>
> Write an 80 to 100 word rejection email. Do not pretend a human reviewed their CV if it was screened automatically. State that their application did not meet the specified requirements for the role without specifying personal details. Include {company_name}'s careers page URL as a placeholder ({careers_page_url}) for the recruiter to insert. No apologies for using automated screening — state it factually. One-sentence close.

**When to use it:** When your ATS auto-reject function does not have a customisable email and you are sending the rejection manually from your inbox.

**Pro tip:** "A human reviewed your application" in a rejection email when no human did is a fast way to earn a hostile reply. The AI will avoid that phrasing if you include the instruction about automated screening.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to candidates who applied for a graduate scheme that has now closed for the current intake.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Scheme name: {scheme_name}
> University or institution attended: {university}
> Application year: {application_year}
> Next intake opening date (if known): {next_intake_date: or write "not yet confirmed"}
> Tone: {tone: encouraging / warm / professional}
>
> Write a 160 to 190 word rejection email. Acknowledge that applying for a graduate scheme takes effort. State the decision clearly. If {next_intake_date} is known, mention it. Do not tell them to "keep working on their skills" without specifics — keep development advice absent unless instructed. Close by pointing them to the company's early careers page ({early_careers_page_url}) as a placeholder. Do not use the phrase "unfortunately, on this occasion."

**When to use it:** At the close of the graduate scheme application window when you have hundreds of rejections to send before the assessment centre invitations go out.

**Pro tip:** "Unfortunately, on this occasion" appears in roughly 80% of rejection emails and candidates have started flagging it in online forums as a signal the email is a copy-paste. Banning it from the prompt produces noticeably better output.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who applied for multiple roles at the same company and was not selected for any of them.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Roles they applied for: {roles_applied: list up to three}
> Number of times they have applied previously: {previous_applications}
> Whether they have been interviewed before: {previously_interviewed: yes / no}
> Tone: {tone: direct / kind / neutral}
>
> Write a 150 to 180 word rejection email that addresses the fact that they have applied for {roles_applied} without stating a blanket ban on future applications. If {previously_interviewed} is yes, acknowledge that directly. If {previous_applications} is three or more, include a single honest sentence about fit without being discouraging. Do not invite them to apply for every future role — suggest they monitor roles in a specific function ({best_fit_function}) instead. No apology for the volume of rejections.

**When to use it:** When your ATS flags a repeat applicant and you want to send a reply that is honest without being dismissive or inviting an endless loop of applications.

**Pro tip:** Repeat applicants often reply to ask what they are doing wrong. If you want to pre-empt that, add "include one sentence offering a 15-minute feedback call if they wish to request one" to the prompt. Only do this if you have the capacity to honour it.

## Rejection Emails After Offer Stage

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who reached offer stage but to whom no offer will be extended due to a failed background check.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Type of check that flagged an issue: {check_type: DBS / credit / employment history verification / reference}
> Legal jurisdiction: {jurisdiction: England and Wales / Scotland / Australia / US state: specify}
> Whether HR has taken legal advice: {legal_advice_taken: yes / no}
> Tone: {tone: formal / factual / neutral}
>
> Write a 160 to 200 word rejection email. Do not specify what the check found. State that the offer is not being extended as a result of information received during the pre-employment screening process. Include a reference to the candidate's right to query the finding with the screening provider (add placeholder {screening_provider_name}). Do not apologise excessively. State the decision is final. No future-opportunity language.

**When to use it:** Before calling the candidate — this email should follow the call, not precede it. Draft it in advance so it is ready to send within 30 minutes of the conversation.

**Pro tip:** If {legal_advice_taken} is no, the AI should not and will not add legal guidance — but review the output before sending and have your employment lawyer clear the language if the check result is a criminal record disclosure. Background check rejections are the highest-risk category for discrimination claims.

> You are an HR manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who declined a verbal offer and has now changed their mind, but the role has been filled.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Date they declined: {decline_date}
> Whether the role has been formally filled: {role_filled: yes / pending start date}
> Relationship quality with candidate: {relationship_quality: strong / neutral / minimal}
> Tone: {tone: direct / warm / regretful}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word email. Acknowledge that circumstances change. State clearly that the role has moved forward and you are unable to reinstate the offer. Do not imply the decision was close or that they "just missed out." If {relationship_quality} is strong, close with a genuine sentence about keeping in contact for future roles. If neutral or minimal, close professionally without future promises. No blame language.

**When to use it:** When a candidate sends the "I've reconsidered" email and you need to respond the same day before they follow up again.
