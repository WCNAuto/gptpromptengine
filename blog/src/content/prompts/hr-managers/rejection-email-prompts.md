## Post-Interview Rejection Emails

> You are an HR Manager writing a post-interview rejection email to a candidate who completed interviews but will not be receiving an offer.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role applied for: {job_title}
> Interview stage reached: {interview_stage: first / second / final}
> Primary reason for rejection (internal use, do not state verbatim): {rejection_reason}
> One genuine strength observed in this candidate: {candidate_strength}
> Tone: {tone: warm / professional / brief}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 150 to 200 word rejection email. Open by thanking the candidate for their time at the {interview_stage} stage. Acknowledge one genuine strength without being vague. Deliver the decision clearly in the second sentence of the second paragraph — do not bury it. Do not give the specific rejection reason. Close with a sincere wish for their search. No invitation to reapply unless explicitly included in {reapply_note}. The email must be sendable as-is.

**When to use it:** Friday afternoon when you have six post-final-round rejections queued and every one of them deserves something better than a form letter.

**Pro tip:** If {rejection_reason} involves a protected characteristic decision (e.g. the hire was a diversity-targeted shortlist), still run the prompt but review the output to confirm the strength mentioned is skill-based, not personality-based. Vague warmth like "you were a joy to meet" has created legal headaches in documented rejection trails.

> You are an HR Manager rejecting a candidate after a final-round panel interview for a senior leadership role.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title} (senior / director / VP level)
> Panel members who met them: {panel_names_or_count}
> Decision margin: {decision_margin: close / clear / unanimous}
> Specific skill area where another candidate was stronger: {differentiating_skill}
> Whether you want to keep them warm for future roles: {keep_warm: yes / no}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 200 to 250 word rejection email for a senior candidate who reached the final panel. Acknowledge the panel specifically. Reference the {differentiating_skill} as a framing for why the decision was difficult without saying they lacked it entirely. If {keep_warm} is yes, include one concrete sentence about staying in contact — not a vague "we'll keep your details". If {keep_warm} is no, close cleanly without that line. Tone is respectful, peer-to-peer. Do not use the phrase "we have decided to move forward with another candidate" — find a more specific construction.

**When to use it:** When rejecting a finalist who came second by a narrow margin and you genuinely want to preserve the relationship for a future role or referral.

**Pro tip:** For senior roles where the candidate knows your CEO or board members personally, paste the output and add a note at the top of the draft: "Flag for [hiring manager] review before send." The prompt produces the copy; the sign-off process is yours to manage.

> You are an HR Manager writing a same-day rejection email to a candidate who completed a first-round phone screen and is not progressing.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Reason not progressing (internal): {screen_out_reason: salary_mismatch / skills_gap / location / overqualified / other}
> Whether the reason is shareable: {shareable: yes / no}
> Recruiter name signing off: {recruiter_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 100 to 130 word rejection email. This is an early-stage rejection — keep it concise and respectful. If {shareable} is yes, include one brief honest sentence about the mismatch. If {shareable} is no, keep the decision brief and unopened. Do not use "we've decided to move forward with candidates whose experience more closely aligns" — that phrase is now widely recognised as hollow. Use direct, human language instead. Sign off from {recruiter_name}.

**When to use it:** The morning after a phone screen when you want to close the loop before the candidate follows up asking for feedback.

**Pro tip:** Salary mismatch is often shareable and candidates appreciate hearing it — it saves them applying to similar roles at your company again. If {screen_out_reason} is salary_mismatch and {shareable} is yes, the prompt will include it. Don't override that instinct to avoid the awkwardness.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who completed a skills-based assessment or take-home task but will not progress to interview.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Task they completed: {task_description}
> Hours the task likely took them: {task_hours}
> Reason not progressing: {rejection_reason: below_threshold / strong_field / skills_gap}
> Feedback shareable: {feedback: yes / no}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 180 to 220 word rejection email. Open by explicitly acknowledging the effort they put into {task_description} — reference the time investment of approximately {task_hours} hours. This is not optional; skipping it after someone completed unpaid work damages your employer brand. Deliver the decision clearly. If {feedback} is yes, include two specific, constructive observations from the task — not compliments, actual observations. If {feedback} is no, do not hint at feedback being available. Close warmly.

**When to use it:** After a high-volume assessment round where 40 candidates completed a task and you're rejecting 35 of them before first interview.

**Pro tip:** If your ATS auto-sends rejections at this stage without a human email, you're already losing candidates to competitor employer brands. This prompt takes 90 seconds to run and the output is meaningfully better than any ATS default message.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to an internal employee who applied for an internal promotion or transfer and will not be selected.
>
> Employee name: {employee_name}
> Role they applied for: {target_role}
> Their current role: {current_role}
> Primary gap that influenced the decision: {development_gap}
> Whether a conversation has already happened in person: {conversation_happened: yes / no}
> Manager who should be looped in: {manager_name}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 180 to 230 word rejection email for an internal candidate. If {conversation_happened} is yes, open by referencing "following our conversation" and treat this email as confirmation, not the primary delivery of news. If {conversation_happened} is no, this email is delivering the news first — open with more care. Reference {development_gap} constructively and include one concrete next step the employee can take to build toward this type of role. CC {manager_name} on the draft. Tone: honest and supportive. Do not use phrases that imply the role is closed to them permanently.

**When to use it:** Within 24 hours of an internal hiring decision, before rumours circulate through the team and the employee hears it secondhand.

**Pro tip:** Never let an internal rejection land via email without the conversation happening first. If {conversation_happened} is no, use this email draft as your prep script for the conversation, then send a lightly modified version afterward as written confirmation.

## High-Volume and Batch Rejection Emails

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email for candidates who applied but were screened out at the CV or application review stage before any contact was made.
>
> Role: {job_title}
> Department: {department}
> Volume of applicants being rejected in this batch: {applicant_volume}
> Whether applications were acknowledged at submission: {acknowledged_at_submission: yes / no}
> Stage of rejection: application review — no interview took place
> Company name: {company_name}
> Recruiter name: {recruiter_name}
>
> Write a 90 to 120 word rejection email for applicants who were not shortlisted for interview. The email must not sound automated even though it will be sent at volume. Use {job_title} and {department} to make it feel role-specific. If {acknowledged_at_submission} is no, acknowledge the gap between application and this email. Do not promise to keep their details on file unless your company policy supports that. Close with a genuine and brief well-wish. No bullet points. Plain prose.

**When to use it:** After closing a job posting that received 200-plus applications and you're sending the bulk reject before announcing the hire internally.

**Pro tip:** Run this once, then paste the output and manually change the opening two words for three or four variations. A/B test open rates in your ATS if it supports it — small copy differences affect whether candidates respond asking for feedback, which costs you time.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email for graduate or entry-level candidates who applied to a competitive scheme and were not selected.
>
> Programme or scheme name: {scheme_name}
> Cohort year: {cohort_year}
> Stage reached: {stage: application / online_test / assessment_centre / final_interview}
> Volume of applicants to this scheme: {total_applicants}
> Number of places offered: {places_offered}
> Specific encouragement note: {encouragement_note}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 160 to 200 word rejection email for a graduate scheme applicant. Contextualise the decision with the competition data ({total_applicants} applied, {places_offered} places). This reduces personalised rejection sting and is genuinely useful information for a 21-year-old building resilience. Include the {encouragement_note} naturally in the close — not as a separate bolted-on paragraph. Tone: warm but not patronising. Do not suggest they "weren't right for us" — focus on the competitive field, not a personal assessment.

**When to use it:** When running a graduate scheme and rejecting the bulk of your assessment centre candidates after offers go out.

**Pro tip:** For {encouragement_note}, write something specific to the stage they reached. "Reaching the assessment centre puts you ahead of 80% of applicants" is more useful than "we were impressed by your enthusiasm." The former gives them something to say in their next interview.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email for candidates who were rejected at a video interview screening stage.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Video platform used: {platform: HireVue / Spark Hire / Async / other}
> Reason for not progressing (internal): {rejection_reason}
> Whether feedback is being offered: {feedback_offered: yes / no / on_request}
> Turnaround time since they submitted: {days_since_submission}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 140 to 170 word rejection email. Acknowledge the specific effort of completing an async video interview — it is more effortful than a form and candidates know it. If {days_since_submission} is more than 10, acknowledge the wait without over-apologising. If {feedback_offered} is yes, explain clearly how they can access it. If {feedback_offered} is on_request, include one sentence inviting them to request it. If {feedback_offered} is no, do not hint that feedback exists internally. Tone: professional and direct.

**When to use it:** After running a video screening round for a role with 80-plus applicants where you've reached a shortlist of 10 and need to notify the other 70.

**Pro tip:** Candidates who complete async video screens and receive no response within two weeks post it on Glassdoor. If {days_since_submission} is over 14, add a one-sentence internal note in your draft flagging this for your employer brand team before you send.

> You are an HR Manager writing a single rejection email that will be sent to all candidates who reached a group assessment centre but were not offered a role.
>
> Assessment centre date: {assessment_date}
> Role or programme: {role_or_programme}
> Activities candidates completed: {activities: group_exercise / case_study / presentation / all}
> Number of candidates at the centre: {candidate_count}
> Number of offers made: {offers_made}
> Next steps for unsuccessful candidates: {next_steps: none / reapply_window / talent_pool}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 200 to 240 word rejection email for candidates who attended a full assessment centre. Reference the date and the specific activities in {activities} so candidates know this is not a generic send. Be honest about the ratio of {offers_made} to {candidate_count}. Include {next_steps} naturally — if none, do not invent false hope. Tone is warm and acknowledges a full day of effort. No bullet points.

**When to use it:** Within 48 hours of assessment centre decisions being finalised, before offers are verbally accepted and word spreads.

**Pro tip:** Assessment centre candidates talk to each other. Send this batch email before anyone has posted on LinkedIn that they received an offer. Being first protects your brand more than being perfect.

> You are an HR Manager writing a rejection email to a candidate who was strong but rejected solely because the role was put on hold or cancelled after they had already been through interviews.
>
> Candidate name: {candidate_name}
> Role: {job_title}
> Stage they reached: {stage}
> Reason role is on hold: {hold_reason: budget_freeze / restructure / headcount_pause / other}
> Whether the role may reopen: {may_reopen: likely / possible / unlikely}
> Whether you want to keep them in a talent pool: {talent_pool: yes / no}
> Company name: {company_name}
>
> Write a 180 to 220 word email. Be explicit that this is not a rejection based on their candidacy — the role itself is the issue. Use {hold_reason} to give an honest one-sentence explanation without revealing confidential business information. If {may_reopen} is likely or possible, give a realistic timeframe if you have one. If {talent_pool} is yes, explain exactly what being in the talent pool means — frequency of contact, who reaches out — not just "we'll keep your details." Tone: transparent and respectful.

**When to use it:** When a restructure or budget freeze kills a role mid-process and you have a strong candidate who deserves a proper explanation.

**Pro tip:** Candidates rejected because a role was cancelled are your highest-conversion future hires when the role reopens — if you handled the communication well. If {talent_pool} is yes, set a calendar reminder to actually contact them within 60 days or the goodwill expires.
